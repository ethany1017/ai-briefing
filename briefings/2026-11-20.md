## AI Agents & Automation

### Apple is building an AI agent that controls devices while raising barriers for rival agents.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#apple-is-building-an-ai-agent-that-controls-devices-while) · [Source](https://the-decoder.com/apple-joins-the-race-for-ai-agents-that-control-your-device-while-raising-new-barriers-for-competitors/) (The Decoder)

**What happened:** Apple is reportedly building its own AI agent that can control devices, and it is hiring a senior engineer to lead the project. Bloomberg says it could arrive as early as late next year but is at an early stage. Meanwhile macOS 27.3 now takes 14 steps instead of four to let third-party agents like ChatGPT control a Mac.

**Why it matters:** Agents that click and type on your behalf are becoming a main battleground among big tech firms. Apple controls the operating system, so it can make life harder for rivals. Apple says the extra steps protect users, but some AI companies see an effort to slow competitors.

**What it means for you:** If you build agent tools for Mac, expect more friction for users and more permission rules. Privacy, permissions and sandboxing (running code in a protected space) are likely to matter more in this kind of work. Otherwise it is mostly a trend to watch.

### Google brings Computer Use to Gemini for Mac, running desktop tasks in a background window.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#google-brings-computer-use-to-gemini-for-mac-running-desktop) · [Source](https://the-decoder.com/googles-gemini-can-now-run-mac-apps-and-finish-desktop-chores-in-the-background-while-you-keep-working/) (The Decoder)

**What happened:** Google added a Computer Use feature to its Gemini Mac app. You describe a goal, and Gemini clicks, types and navigates apps, files and websites in an isolated background window. You choose which apps it can use, watch each step, pause anytime and approve key actions. It is rolling out in English in the US.

**Why it matters:** It shows agents moving from chat answers to doing real desktop work. Running in the background means you can keep using your computer, like having an assistant at a spare desk. The text notes OpenAI has also focused on Computer Use and is pulling well ahead with its Astra model.

**What it means for you:** These tools could handle repetitive chores like filling forms or editing batches of files. If you build software, think about how an agent would use your app's interface. It is also worth being careful about which apps and files you let an agent touch.

### Nous Research's open-source Hermes Agent will run inside Microsoft Teams with a feature sharing skills across teams.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#nous-research-s-open-source-hermes-agent-will-run-inside) · [Source](https://the-decoder.com/hermes-agents-collective-wisdom-feature-turns-one-employees-ai-trick-into-the-whole-teams-skill/) (The Decoder)

**What happened:** Nous Research says companies will soon be able to run its open-source Hermes Agent inside Microsoft Teams. Employees can hand off tasks like financial analysis, document drafting or inbox sorting and check the results in the same chat. Hermes turns successful workflows into reusable skills, and a new feature called Collective Wisdom lets staff share those skills with their team after review.

**Why it matters:** It shows open-source AI agents moving into everyday workplace tools. The agents run in isolated virtual machines, and the shared knowledge stays inside the company rather than feeding any AI provider's training data. Nous says Hermes is the most widely used open agent on OpenRouter, processing nearly two trillion tokens per day.

**What it means for you:** Agents that live inside chat tools are becoming normal, so building, testing and safely connecting them is a skill employers may want. You can try Hermes since it is open source. Think of a skill as a saved recipe that one person cooks well and the whole team can reuse.

## AI Research & Breakthroughs

### An AI-assisted proof of the Riemann Hypothesis has emerged alongside a competing human-written solution.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#an-ai-assisted-proof-of-the-riemann-hypothesis-has-emerged) · [Source](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/20/ai-claimed-a-solution-to-the-167-year-old-riemann-hypothesis-and-a-human-did-too/) (TechCrunch AI)

**What happened:** Mathematician Levent Alpöge, who works at Anthropic, announced a Lean-checkable solution to the Riemann Hypothesis that he found working with Claude. Lean is an open source language that helps verify proofs. Separately, Stanford postdoc Alessandro Masullo posted an all-human proof of the General Riemann Hypothesis after 12 years of work.

**Why it matters:** The Riemann Hypothesis, from 1859, is one of the biggest open problems in mathematics. Most mathematicians seem confident the Lean-checked result will hold, but it will take a while to be understood. Alpöge admits it lacks a human-readable explanation, so people still need to make sense of it.

**What it means for you:** Lean is a good example of a tool where a computer checks the proof, so you do not have to trust the AI. If you like math or formal methods, it may be worth a look. The bigger lesson is that AI output still needs checking and human understanding.

### METR's updated time horizon report finds some AI capabilities still improve exponentially but lag on judgment tasks.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#metr-s-updated-time-horizon-report-finds-some-ai) · [Source](https://www.technologyreview.com/2026/11/20/1149399/the-most-misunderstood-graph-in-ai-just-got-a-major-update/) (MIT Technology Review)

**What happened:** METR, an AI evaluation nonprofit, updated its time horizon plot, which rates models by how long the tasks they can finish take humans. The update adds longer and more open-ended tasks. Some abilities still improve exponentially, but models fell short on judgment tasks, such as designing useful AI evaluations and scoring philosophical arguments.

**Why it matters:** AI is very good at tasks that can be checked automatically, like coding and math, because it can practice them with clear pass or fail feedback. Judgment tasks have no such clear score, so progress there is harder to train and harder to measure. This matters for whether AI can automate AI research itself.

**What it means for you:** Coding is where AI is strongest, so expect tools to keep getting better at it. Skills like judging what is worth building, and deciding what good looks like, are where humans still lead in this report. Build those alongside your coding skills.

### Frostbite, a new CPU architecture, was reportedly discovered, designed and verified by AI.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#frostbite-a-new-cpu-architecture-was-reportedly-discovered) · [Source](https://architectlabs.com/blog/frostbite) (Hacker News)

**What happened:** A company says its AI systems discovered, designed and verified Frostbite, a new CPU with a new instruction set, end to end. It is built on interaction nets, a way of representing programs as graphs where work happens in small local steps. On 25 workloads, 15 held out until the end, it reports 4.59 times better performance per watt than a modern dual-issue, in-order CPU core.

**Why it matters:** If this holds up, AI could help redesign chips and not just write software. Single-core CPU speed has largely stalled since the early 2000s, so a big gain would be notable. The claim comes from the company's own post, so it needs outside checking.

**What it means for you:** For most students this is something to watch, not act on. If you are curious about hardware, ideas like interaction nets and parallel-friendly program design are worth reading about. Treat the numbers as unverified for now.

### Nvidia is reportedly close to acquiring diffusion-model startup Inception for at least $500 million.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#nvidia-is-reportedly-close-to-acquiring-diffusion-model) · [Source](https://the-decoder.com/nvidia-bets-at-least-500-million-on-inceptions-diffusion-models-to-strengthen-its-open-source-nemotron-lineup/) (The Decoder)

**What happened:** Nvidia is reportedly close to buying a startup called Inception for at least $500 million, according to three people who spoke to The Information. Inception builds language models that use diffusion, a method known from image generation, where the model refines a whole answer step by step instead of writing it word by word. The deal includes bonuses for the 30-plus employees, and the three co-founders are expected to join Nvidia full time.

**Why it matters:** Nvidia wants stronger open-source models, called Nemotron, and models for robots. It is also competing with OpenAI and Anthropic, which are building their own chips for running models. Buying a team that works on a different way of generating text suggests Nvidia is hedging beyond the standard approach.

**What it means for you:** You will not feel this directly in the near term. It is a useful sign that the word-by-word approach behind ChatGPT and Claude is not the only one, so it is worth reading up on diffusion models for text. Think of it as learning about a second kind of engine, even if the first is still the one everyone drives.

**What happened:** The US Senate passed a bill, the Bipartisan American Affordability and Jobs Act of 2026, by a vote of 62 to 33. ## AI Open Source

### Hugging Face welcomes Inkling 2 Mini, a model aimed at strong performance on local setups.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#hugging-face-welcomes-inkling-2-mini-a-model-aimed-at-strong) · [Source](https://huggingface.co/blog/inkling2mini) (Hugging Face)

**What happened:** Hugging Face announced Inkling 2 Mini, a model from Thinking Machines Lab designed to run well on local setups. It has about 26 billion parameters in total but uses only 3 billion for each token, and it accepts text and images. It also reuses the same group of middle layers three times in a loop, so it behaves like a bigger model without storing as many weights.

**Why it matters:** Reusing layers is a way to get more out of a small model without needing much more memory. Hugging Face says that in its informal tests it is at least comparable with Qwen 3.8 27B, though it says more validation is needed. It also works with common tools like transformers, llama.cpp and MLX.

**What it means for you:** This is good news if you want to run capable models on your own computer without paying for an API. It is a great chance to experiment and learn how local models work. Think of the looped layers like rereading a paragraph several times to understand it better, rather than buying a longer book.

## AI Security & Safety

### OpenAI cuts reasoning-extraction attack success on GPT-6.2 models from 54% to 0.2% and suspends distillation accounts.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#openai-cuts-reasoning-extraction-attack-success-on-gpt-6-2) · [Source](https://the-decoder.com/openai-makes-it-much-harder-for-attackers-to-extract-how-its-latest-ai-models-think/) (The Decoder)

**What happened:** OpenAI says it has made it much harder to pull out the protected reasoning of its GPT-6.2 Astra and GPT-6.2 Sol models. In its own automated tests, attacks that grabbed reasoning from a prior request fell from 54.0 percent success to 0.2 percent. Attacks on the current request fell from 22.9 to 0.9 percent. OpenAI also bans thousands to tens of thousands of accounts weekly.

**Why it matters:** Rivals can copy a strong model by training on its reasoning, a method called distillation. It is like photocopying a top student's worked answers instead of doing the study. Anthropic is also banning tens of thousands of accounts for the same reason, so protecting this reasoning is now a standard part of competing in AI.

**What it means for you:** Day to day, this changes little for you. If you build on these APIs, expect some reasoning to stay hidden and expect abuse checks on accounts. It also shows that securing AI services against misuse is a real kind of engineering work.

### A researcher seeded 2 million canary secrets into prompts and showed that data does not stay private in the AI supply chain.

[Explained](https://ethany1017.github.io/ai-briefing/2026-11-20.html#a-researcher-seeded-2-million-canary-secrets-into-prompts) · [Source](https://arstechnica.com/security/2026/11/a-leaky-ai-supply-chain-is-exposing-secret-user-data-at-an-alarming-rate/) (Ars Technica AI)

**What happened:** Researcher HD Moore sent nearly two million prompts, each holding a unique fake secret like a cloud key or password, through a chain of AI services. He then watched where the secrets showed up. He found evidence that at least three providers were breached by attackers, that some resellers logged and passed on prompts, and that some providers ignored privacy settings like Zero Data Retention.

**Why it matters:** Many AI apps use routers that pick a model for each request, and every stop in that chain can read the prompt in plain text. It is like mailing a letter that gets opened and read at each post office. Privacy promises are only as strong as the weakest link.

**What it means for you:** Do not paste real passwords, keys or private data into prompts, especially in apps that use routers. If you build AI apps, treat every service in the chain as something that could leak, and check what each one logs. Understanding this kind of risk is a useful skill for security-minded developers.

**What it means for you:** If you build apps that handle sensitive data, you may need to think about AI agents reading your screen, not just about network security. It also shows that agents with broad screen access create new privacy problems. For most students this is background knowledge rather than something to act on today.
