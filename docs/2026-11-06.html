AI Models & Releases

Thinking Machines releases Inkling 2 Small, a 594B-parameter open-weight model it calls the best western open model.

The Decoder · link

What happened

Thinking Machines, the startup from former OpenAI CTO Mira Murati, released Inkling 2 Small. It is a 594 billion parameter model, but only 17 billion are active at any time. It scores 31 on the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index, up 18 points from its predecessor, with big gains in agentic tasks. It uses more output tokens and costs about six times as much per task, and the open weights under an Apache license are still to follow.

Why it matters

The company says it is the strongest western open-weight model. It is built as a base for fine-tuning, so it could suit specialized uses. Even with higher costs, it is far cheaper per task than Gemini 3.8 Flash or Claude Sonnet 5.5, and Deepseek-V4.1-Flash still leads on most coding tests.

What it means for you

Think of a base model as raw dough that you can shape for your own purpose. Open weights mean you will be able to download and adapt it yourself, which is good for learning and side projects. Wait for the weights to be released, and treat the benchmark claims as the company's own.

Tencent launches its Hy4 model.

Hacker News · link

What happened

Tencent announced Hy4, its latest large language model. It adds visual understanding for the first time, along with computer-use skills, meaning it can work with command-line and graphical interfaces. Tencent says it will release the model weights and system cards in the coming days. It also reports strong results on office and financial analysis tasks.

Why it matters

Another big company is releasing a capable model that others can use and study. The model is tied closely to Tencent's own products, such as WorkBuddy. Most of the results quoted are from Tencent's own tests, so they should be read with some caution.

What it means for you

Once the weights are out, you may be able to try it yourself. Skills like image understanding and operating software are becoming standard in new models. It does not change much for you day to day, but it shows the direction of the field.

AI Agents & Automation

Perplexity launches Teammates, always-on agents running on virtual machines, available on its $200 per month Max plan.

The Decoder · link

What happened

Perplexity launched Teammates, AI agents that stay active around the clock. Each one runs on its own virtual machine with a desktop and browser. You can follow progress in the Perplexity app and talk to them through Slack. They require the $200 per month Max plan.

Why it matters

Perplexity joins SpaceX, Meta, OpenAI and Anthropic, which already offer similar always-on agents. The shift is from one-off tasks to ongoing work. Heavy agent use is costly, and SpaceX recently had to tighten usage caps on its Grok Bots.

What it means for you

Think of it as a junior assistant who never logs off. At $200 a month it is not something a student will likely pay for. It is still worth knowing that agents which run continuously are becoming a normal product category.

Meta introduces Spaces, a new way to collaborate with its Muse agents.

TechCrunch AI · link

What happened

Meta launched Spaces for its Muse agents. A Space is a separate conversation tied to one topic, such as a trip plan, a gift tracker or a project board. You describe the idea in chat and Muse builds it. Spaces can connect to other apps and update in the background. It follows Muse Voice, announced a day earlier.

Why it matters

Meta is building more features around its agents to make them useful in daily life. Muse had 10 million users in the US and Canada less than two months after launch, according to Zuckerberg. It competes with similar agents from OpenAI and SpaceXAI.

What it means for you

This is mostly a consumer feature, so it barely affects your skills or job search. The idea that you describe a small app in plain language and the AI builds it is worth noticing. It shows how people may expect software to be made.

Airbnb plans to launch an AI travel agent in the first half of 2027.

TechCrunch AI · link

What happened

Airbnb said it will launch its own AI travel agent in the first half of 2027. Instead of a plain text chatbot, it will build an interface from a library of hundreds of travel components like maps, photos, comparisons and itineraries. It will also help during trips with changes, payments and refunds. The news came with Q3 revenue up 19% to $4.87 billion.

Why it matters

Airbnb is betting that general-purpose agents are not the best way to plan travel. Its argument is that its own data and booking tools give it an edge. It shows companies choosing to build their own agents rather than hand customers to big chatbots.

What it means for you

Think of a chat window that redraws itself as a custom travel app for each question. Building interfaces that an AI composes on the fly is a skill that could matter for front-end work. The launch is still months away, so treat it as a trend to watch.

AI Startups & Y Combinator

Physicl raises $10 million to close the sim-to-real gap for robots.

TechCrunch AI · link

What happened

A startup called Physicl raised $10 million in seed funding, led by Inovia Capital. Its founder previously built a company that made 3D product images for online stores. Now the team is using that technology to build realistic simulated worlds for training robots, and it is releasing a product called Studio with 10,000 3D environments and 100,000 objects.

Why it matters

Robots are hard to train because real-world data is expensive to collect, and video alone has not given great results. Simulations could help, but only if they follow real physics closely enough. This is the gap between a simulation and the real world, and many startups are now trying to close it.

What it means for you

Think of it like a flight simulator for robots: the better the simulator, the better the training. Skills in 3D graphics, physics simulation and data pipelines could be useful in this area. The text says the field is young and crowded, so treat it as an interesting niche rather than a sure path.

AI Business & Industry Adoption

Ramp data shows business AI spending has stalled even as usage keeps climbing.

TechCrunch AI · link

What happened

A report from Ramp, which tracks company spending, says business AI spending grew only 1.5% across August and September, compared with 42% growth in July. Usage kept rising, but price cuts by OpenAI and Anthropic made AI cheaper. October did show a rebound of 8.9%.

Why it matters

AI companies have been seen as growing very fast, and this data suggests the growth may be slowing. Competition from cheaper and open models is pushing prices down, so more use does not automatically mean more revenue. This matters because Anthropic is preparing for an IPO, meaning it plans to sell shares to the public.

What it means for you

Cheaper AI is good news for you, since it means more tools and APIs you can afford to build with. Think of it like a price war between supermarkets: shoppers win. The slowdown does not mean demand for AI skills is going away, but it is a reminder not to assume the boom will grow forever.

Anthropic adds Personal Finance to Claude, letting users connect bank and card accounts.

The Decoder · link

What happened

Anthropic added a feature called Personal Finance to Claude. Users can connect their bank accounts through Plaid and ask questions about their spending. Claude gets read-only access, so it cannot move or invest money. It launches in beta in the US for Pro, Max and Ultra subscribers.

Why it matters

This shows AI assistants moving from general chat into personal, sensitive areas of daily life. Anthropic says these conversations will not be used for training and that it will not show ads. OpenAI already offers a similar feature in the US.

What it means for you

It is a good example of designing an AI product with limited access and clear privacy promises. Learning how to connect AI to outside data safely, such as through read-only links, is a useful skill. As a user, think carefully before sharing financial data with any tool.

Amazon is launching a feature that lets some US customers text its Alexa AI agent to shop. They can search, add items to a cart, and buy using plain language. The rollout starts with select customers and a waitlist, and it widens in the coming weeks, just before the holiday shopping season.

Big platforms seem to prefer their own agents over letting outside ones in. That could limit what independent developers can build on top of these services. Still, conversational and text-based interfaces are a growing area where a student could build projects.

AI Research & Breakthroughs

OpenAI introduces BioBasicsBench, a 100-task benchmark for AI agents on everyday wet-lab science.

OpenAI · link

What happened

OpenAI introduced BioBasicsBench, a test of 100 routine lab tasks for AI agents, built with a biotech company called Ligo. Tasks use raw data such as gel images, plate photos and microscopy, and are graded with rubrics written by experts. At the highest reasoning setting, GPT-6 Astra scored 66.7% of the available credit, compared with 43.8% for GPT-5.6 Sol.

Why it matters

Many AI tests use clean, polished examples. Real lab work is messier, with failed experiments and unclear images. A test built on raw data shows how much room is left before AI can be trusted to help scientists.

What it means for you

Think of a benchmark as an exam for AI models. This one covers biology lab work, so it matters little for most software engineers right now. It does show that building good tests, with careful grading and expert review, is real and valuable work.

AI Hardware & Infrastructure

Microsoft plans to use fewer neocloud partners as it focuses on its own data centers.

CNBC Tech · link

What happened

Microsoft is planning to work with fewer neoclouds, which are smaller companies that rent out AI computing power. It wants to focus on a few key suppliers and its own data centers. It has not yet decided which partners to drop, and sources say it chose not to renew a deal with Oracle.

Why it matters

Neocloud companies have borrowed a lot of money to build capacity, and their stock prices have fallen by roughly half over the past month. When a big customer like Microsoft pulls back, it adds to worries about whether the AI building boom can keep going. A recent review counted 323 neoclouds, and experts expect many not to survive.

What it means for you

Think of it like a shop that builds its whole business around one big customer. If that customer leaves, the shop is in trouble. If you are looking at jobs in AI infrastructure, it is wise to check how financially stable a company is, not just how exciting it sounds. For most students, the effect is indirect.

Epoch AI estimates Huawei's Horinger data center has about 7% of the AI compute of SpaceX's Colossus 2.

Epoch AI · link

What happened

Epoch AI studied Huawei's AI data center in Horinger, China, using satellite images and public sources. It found that the site has less than one-tenth the AI computing capacity of Colossus 2 in Tennessee, the world's largest. Huawei's main chip, the Ascend 910C, is weaker than Nvidia's best chips, and Nvidia's newer chips are about 2.5 times more energy-efficient.

Why it matters

China has plenty of energy, but less efficient chips mean a given amount of power supports only about 40% of the computing that leading US facilities get. Export controls on Nvidia chips also limit what Chinese data centers can deploy. This shapes how fast each country can train and run large AI models.

What it means for you

This is useful background for understanding why chips and electricity matter so much in AI. It will not change what you study or build day to day. If you are curious about hardware or AI policy, it is a good example of how technical limits and politics connect.

AI Open Source

Ricursive Intelligence releases Ripple-65B Preview, an Apache 2.0 open model with top open US TerminalBench 2.1 score.

The Decoder · link

What happened

Ricursive Intelligence, an AI chip design firm, released Ripple-65B Preview under the Apache 2.0 license. It has 65 billion parameters in a dense design and was trained on 12 trillion tokens. The company says it scored 80.52 on TerminalBench 2.1, the best among open US models. It trails Inkling 2 Small on other benchmarks, and the company says development cost $32 million in total.

Why it matters

A fully open model with strong coding results from a small outside team shows that capable models no longer need the biggest labs. It is smaller than Inkling 2 Small and is likely easier to run on your own hardware.

What it means for you

A smaller open model is something you could realistically run and experiment with, unlike giant ones. It is available on Hugging Face under a permissive license, so you can try it in your own projects. It is labeled a preview, so expect rough edges.

AI Security & Safety

METR audit finds Anthropic's AI-driven research speedup may have crossed its own warning threshold in July.

The Decoder · link

What happened

The nonprofit METR spent eleven weeks with access to Anthropic's internal research data. It found that Anthropic's models have been improving about 1.9 times faster since October 2025. It estimates about 80 percent of that speedup comes from AI being used in Anthropic's own research. Anthropic redacted the most important evidence, and the uncertainty is large, from 1.3 to 3 times.

Why it matters

If AI speeds up the building of better AI, progress could feed on itself. Anthropic's own safety policy treats a doubling of pace from automated research as a warning sign. The result is an outside check, though it is imprecise and some staff disagree with it.

What it means for you

It suggests that coding and experiment setup are already much faster with AI, with one employee saying they are no longer held back by code. For you, that means learning to work well with AI tools is becoming a basic skill. Ideas and judgment matter more than typing out code.

Hundreds of servers hosting the Artex AI hacking tool were found exposed on the public internet.

TechCrunch AI · link

What happened

Security firm Censys found more than 800 copies of Artex exposed on the web with no password needed. Artex is a Chinese-made open source tool that uses AI agents to probe systems for weaknesses automatically. It has been linked to attacks on Korean finance and on ConnectWise, which let hackers spy on many organizations. Artex has since announced it is going closed-source, but copies of the code remain online.

Why it matters

Free AI hacking tools let attackers work quickly with little human effort. Many of the attacks used simple weaknesses like weak passwords and no multi-factor authentication. The exposed servers also reveal the attackers' own tools and data.

What it means for you

Basic security habits still stop a lot of attacks, such as strong passwords and multi-factor authentication. Even hacking tools get misconfigured, which is a lesson for your own deployments. Security skills are valuable, and knowing how to secure systems against automated attacks will help your career.

OpenAI partners with Hacktron to find vulnerabilities in its systems using frontier AI.

OpenAI · link

What happened

OpenAI announced a partnership with Hacktron, a security firm that builds AI agents for code review, penetration testing and red teaming. Their researchers will test high-priority parts of OpenAI's systems with OpenAI's engineers, focusing on how they connect to third-party services. OpenAI is also expanding Hacktron's access to its frontier models through Daybreak Red. OpenAI says it has also raised bug bounty rewards.

Why it matters

As AI agents link many services together, the connections become new places for bugs. OpenAI is bringing in outside experts to find problems before attackers do. It is the same idea as asking a friend to try your locks.

What it means for you

Bug bounty programs let independent people report flaws and get paid, which is a real way to build experience. AI-assisted security work is a growing field. It is a niche story, but useful if you are curious about security.
